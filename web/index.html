A sequencer that plays like a board game. Firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1.

⚠ Not yet tested on a real FM-1. Every change to RoboSeq is built and tested on a computer and in an FM-1 emulator, but it has never run on an actual device. Install it at your own risk.

Testers wanted. If you have an FM-1 and know how to restore it (see below the Install button), you could be the first to play RoboSeq on real hardware. There is a half-hour checklist for the first install. Afterwards, open an issue on GitHub and tell us how it went: what worked, what didn't, what the screen showed and how it sounded.

Two robots, Scout-7 and Echo-3, move around a 5 × 5 board of conveyor belts, zap rails, rotators, pulse pads and crates. Each robot follows its own program of moves, and everything they do becomes music: where they go, what they ride and what they bump into. You play RoboSeq by changing things while it runs. Edit their steps on the white keys, type in a new program, change the board, give a robot a one-off nudge with the black keys, or play a solo over the top. Or give a friend the other end of the keyboard and control the robots yourselves, in a battle on the beat.

2robots, each with a program of up to 64 steps
6preset boards, plus random ones and your own, built while it plays
73named chord progressions, in six keys
2players in battle mode, one robot each

Hear it

Here is RoboSeq playing its default song, once in each of its two engines. Nothing is touched: the robots just run their programs. The recordings come from the firmware's own code, run on a computer. Click the picture under a demo to jump to that point.

You switch engines with SELECT on the GLO page. BAND is the default. ORIGINAL is tested against the browser version: all 88,120 notes in 36 test runs match.

Install

  1. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer (they can talk to the FM-1 over USB MIDI).
  2. Connect the FM-1 directly to the computer with a USB data cable, and switch it on.
  3. Close other apps that use MIDI, tick the box, press Install and allow MIDI when the browser asks.

Log

  

  
This page installs the latest build of the main branch, which has passed all its
    tests. Don't unplug the cable while it is writing (about a minute). If an install is interrupted, the FM-1 stays in
    update mode: plug it back in and press Install again to finish. If the FM-1 doesn't start at all, hold
    OCT− while you switch it on. It then starts in its USB rescue mode, where installing always works. RoboSeq
    keeps its project in its own part of the flash memory, and never reads or changes another firmware's projects or
    samples. To go back, install any firmware from the FM-1 Firmware Hub, or
    the official one with M-VAVE's own updater, M-UPGRADE. Installing firmware is at your own risk.


  
Meet the robots

  

    

      
      
Scout-7

      
The red one. Scout-7 starts in the bottom-left corner, facing right, and its program is shown down the left
        side of the board. In BAND it plays the main tune, which comes back every bar. In ORIGINAL it plays a note on
        every step, depending on where it stands.

      
Nudge it with F#3 G#3 A#3 C#4 D#4. Each key replaces its next move with one
        of these, just once, and then it carries on with its program.

      

        
        
        
      

      
In battle mode it has no program, and a player controls it: F3 F#3 G3 A3 to
        move, C4 to strike.

    

    

      
      
Echo-3

      
The orange one. Echo-3 starts in the top-right corner, facing left, and its program is shown down the right
        side of the board. In BAND it answers Scout-7, then sings along with it. In ORIGINAL it plays a harmony to
        Scout-7 on every step, plus the bass.

      
Nudge it with F#4 G#4 A#4 C#5 D#5. Each key replaces its next move with one
        of these, just once, and then it carries on with its program.

      

        
        
        
      

      
In battle mode it has no program, and a player controls it: C5 C#5 D5 E5 to
        move, G5 to strike.

    

  

  
The last black key, F#5, swaps their programs, which changes who plays
    high. A nudge only lasts until the end of the loop. Then the robots go back to their starting pads and follow their
    programs again.


  
Their world

  
The moves a robot can make, one for each step of its program, as they look on the screen:

  

    
Moveone tile forward, unless a wall or a crate is in the way

    
Backone tile back

    
Turn La quarter turn left

    
Turn Ra quarter turn right

    
Dashtwo tiles; landing on the other robot hits it

    
Pinga pulse forward that heats up the other robot

    
Waitstands still and lets the tiles play

  

  
The tiles, which you can place with the board builder while it plays:

  

    
Beltcarries a robot along

    
Rotator Lturns a robot left

    
Rotator Rturns a robot right

    
Zap railheats a robot up

    
Pulse padcools a robot down

    
Crateblocks the way

  

  
A robot that reaches heat 6 burns out and comes back in the farthest corner. If both robots stay on
    the same tile for two steps in a row, they fall in love and a coin sound plays. In BAND every board sounds
    different: each has its own bass groove, and its tiles shape the notes. Pulse pads give long notes with vibrato,
    zap rails short hard ones, belts arpeggios and rotators pitch bends, and crates cut a note off.


  
Playing it

  

    
PLAYStarts the board from the beginning, or stops it. It flashes in time with the beat.

    
White keysOn HOME, these are the selected robot's steps: press a key to change its step to the
      next move. SELECT chooses what the keys change: the move, its modifier (steady, bright, low, wobble, glitch) or
      its chance of playing (100, 75 or 50 %).

    
Black keysNudge the robots: five keys for each robot, and one to swap their programs.

    
EDITChooses whose program is on the white keys: Scout-7, or Echo-3 when it is lit.

    
SEQShows both programs as step cards. Type moves in with the keys laid out as A W S D, as in a
      game.

    
SELVoice Mod, which sets the chords. Press it again for Seq 2, the arrangement. In BAND a random
      roll picks one of 73 named chord progressions, and FOLLOW picks a new one each time the last one comes
      round.

    
ENVAll the boards, with the next one framed until the loop comes round. Press it again for the
      board builder, where you place tiles under the robots while they move.

    
LFO · FXThe drums, one row at a time on the white keys; and the mixer, with the levels on
      KNOB1.

    
GLOTempo, ticks per move, loop length and speed on the knobs. SELECT switches the engine, and
      white key 16 starts battle mode.

    
REC · ARPAll 27 keys play a SID-style lead over the robots, with a ping-pong echo in time. ARP
      turns a held chord into an arpeggio.

    
SAVELights up when there are unsaved changes. RoboSeq saves by itself once it is stopped; press
      SAVE to save right away.

    
K1–K4The values on the current page, shown on the screen. Turning them never stops the
      music.

The battle

  
  
Press GLO, then white key 16. Each player takes a robot and one end of the keyboard, and there are no programs:
    hold a direction and your robot takes a step that way on every beat of the music. Strike when the other robot is in
    reach. A hit adds heat and knocks the two apart, and a robot that reaches heat 6 burns out and loses the round. If
    a round goes on too long, the arena starts to overheat from the edges. PLAY counts in 4, 3, 2, 1 on the beat, and
    the first to win the set number of rounds (1 to 9, set with K2) wins the match.

  

    
LeftUpDownRightStrike

    
Scout-7F3F#3G3A3C4

    
Echo-3C5C#5D5E5G5

  

  
F#4, in the middle between the players, restarts the match. When the other robot is out of reach,
    the strike key plays a lead note from the chord instead, depending on where you stand.

  

    
    
    
    
  

  

  
Try it without an FM-1

  
You can run the firmware itself in fm1-emu,
    an FM-1 emulator that runs in the browser. Download the file below, click Open .fwsc… in the emulator (or
    drag the file onto it) and press Start. Then play it with your computer keyboard, the mouse, a touch screen or
    a MIDI keyboard.

  
RoboSeq started out as an instrument in the browser, and that original is still the reference for how the
    firmware should sound and behave. You can play it at jhf.no.
