A sequencer that is a board game, firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1.

⚠ Not yet tried on a real FM-1. RoboSeq is built and tested on every change, on a computer and in the FM-1 emulator, but it has not been tried on an actual device. Install it at your own risk.

Testers wanted. Have an FM-1, and know how to get it back (below the Install button)? Be the first to play RoboSeq on one: the first flash is a half-hour checklist, and an issue on GitHub is where to tell how it went: what worked, what did not, what the screen showed and how it sounded.

Two robots, Scout-7 and Echo-3, walk a 5 × 5 board of conveyor belts, zap rails, rotators, pulse pads and crates, each running a program of moves, and where they go, what they ride and what they bump into is the music. Their programs decide how they move, and you play it by changing them while it runs: turn their steps on the white keys, type a program in, change their board, nudge a robot off its program for a step on the black keys, solo over them. Or hand a friend the other end of the keyboard and steer them yourselves, in a battle on the beat.

2robots, each a program of up to 64 steps
6boards, a random roll and your own, built while it plays
73named chord progressions, in six keys
2players in the battle, a robot each, on the beat

Hear it

Both demos are the firmware itself, played on a computer as it sends its sound over USB: the same C as on the FM-1, the robots left alone on the board it starts with. The picture under each is its spectrum, low to high, from start to end: tap it to jump.

RoboSeq has two engines, and SELECT on GLO picks one. BAND is where it starts. ORIGINAL plays the browser original's score, checked note for note: 88,120 notes over 36 scenarios.

Install

  1. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer (they can talk to the FM-1 over USB MIDI).
  2. Connect the FM-1 directly to the computer with a USB data cable, and switch it on.
  3. Close other apps that use MIDI, tick the box, press Install and allow MIDI when the browser asks.

Log

  

  
This page installs the latest build of main, the one its tests passed. Do not unplug
    the cable while it writes (about a minute). If an install is cut off, the FM-1 stays in update mode: replug it and
    press Install again, and it finishes. If the FM-1 does not start at all, hold OCT− while switching it on: it
    starts in the USB rescue, where an installer always works. RoboSeq keeps its project in flash sectors of its own
    and does not read or write another firmware's projects or samples. To go back, install any firmware from the
    FM-1 Firmware Hub, or the official one with M-VAVE's own updater
    M-UPGRADE. Installing firmware is at your own risk.

Heat 6 burns a robot out, and it comes back in the corner farthest away. Two on one tile, two steps
    running, fall in love and play the coin. In BAND each board is a band of its own: its bass groove, and its tiles
    shape the notes (pulse pads long ones with vibrato, zap rails short hard ones, belts arpeggios, rotators bends,
    crates stop a note dead).


  
Playing it

  

    
PLAYStarts the board from its beginning, or stops. It flashes the beat.

    
White keysOn HOME, the picked robot's steps: a key turns its step to the next move. SELECT picks
      what it turns: the move, its modifier (steady, bright, low, wobble, glitch) or its chance (100, 75, 50 %).

    
Black keysNudge the robots, two hands of five, and the swap.

    
EDITWhose program is on the white keys: Scout-7, or Echo-3 when lit.

    
SEQBoth programs as step cards. Type moves in as you would drive: A W S D, and on.

    
SELVoice Mod, the chords; again, Seq 2, the arrangement. In BAND a roll is one of 73 named
      progressions, and FOLLOW has a new one follow each time it comes round.

    
ENVThe boards, the one coming framed as the loop comes round; again, the builder: lay tiles under
      the robots while they walk.

    
LFO · FXThe drums, a row on the white keys; the mix, its faders on KNOB1.

    
GLOTempo, ticks per move, the loop's length and the speed on the knobs; SELECT the engine;
      white key 16 the battle.

    
REC · ARPAll 27 keys play a SID lead over the robots, with a ping-pong echo in time; ARP
      arpeggiates a held chord.

    
SAVELit while there are changes not kept yet. Stopped, RoboSeq keeps its project by itself;
      SAVE keeps it at once.

    
K1–K4The page's values, shown on the screen. They never stop the music.

The battle

  
  
GLO, then white key 16. Each player takes a robot and an end of the keyboard, and there is no program: hold a way
    and your robot steps there on every beat of the music, strike when the other is in reach. A hit is heat, and it
    drives the two apart; at heat 6 a robot burns out and the other takes the round. A long round overheats the arena
    from its edge. PLAY counts 4, 3, 2, 1 on the beats, and the first to the rounds (1 to 9, on K2) wins.

F#4, in the middle between you, starts the match again. Out of reach, the strike key plays the lead,
    a tone of the chord where you stand.

    keyboard.
